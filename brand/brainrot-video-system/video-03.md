33 seconds · 1080×1920 · 30fps · Full-release launch promo

Read [fact matrix](00-FACT-MATRIX.md), [production bible](01-PRODUCTION-BIBLE.md) and [launch assumptions](02-LAUNCH-ASSUMPTIONS.md) with this package. Machine-readable timings and prompts are in [production-data.json](production-data.json).

## A. Concept

The Don demands a rat in an ugly ultra-wide phone call. His AI adviser points him toward a giant public shipping notice. Tabloid photos crash into a fluorescent copyshop, independent source checks and an opaque city window that refuses the viewer private information. Two players contribute through separate terminals, the Don approves and the authorized Auditor executes. A city lightbox carries the persistent result. The adviser is a dry human-form call persona, and the joke is that everyone wants a verdict while the game only supports a recorded disclosure.

**Factual boundary:** F02–F09, F17, F20–F23, F26–F27, F43–F47. Full-release master under L01, L04 and L05: assume the adviser is integrated with the released campaign and command services. Its performed lines illustrate permitted reasoning rather than impersonating an actual model-call transcript. The tested campaign loop predates the opening: a route was diverted, a market established and exposed, enabling this investigation. No automatic culprit or betrayal detector exists. Not every outcome spawns a new opportunity.

## B. Viral thesis

- **recognition:** Tabloid accusations, video calls, screenshot arguments, private windows, investigation games.
- **novelty:** The AI adviser is the only person refusing to turn a public notice into a guilty verdict.
- **sensation:** Phone distortion → huge public notice → fluorescent paper snap → deny tone → city lightbox.
- **pattern interrupt:** Ugly ultra-wide Don phone face: “Find the rat.” Blank phone fills lower foreground.
- **information gap:** Why is the suspected leak public, and why can two matching records still fail to establish guilt?
- **share trigger:** The friend who mistakes two screenshots for two sources; the adviser says no.
