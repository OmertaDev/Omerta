## Liquidity story: keep the two fee paths distinct

1. A player confirms a canonical **OMR → ETH** swap. Game-cash earnings do not automatically become OMR.
2. The applicable **Hook fee's POL allocation funds Core** through its configured funding adapter. Other configured buckets are not silently reassigned to the Family.
3. **LP fees from the designated seasonal Turf range** reach its own fee bridge. They are distinct from the Hook tax and Core funding.
4. During a siege, actual earned/funded fees enter its escrow under the configured rules. Victory does not imply that the attacker receives every escrowed unit.
5. Typed game settlement checkpoints the lane at the ownership transition. Previously credited fees do not retroactively change owner.
6. After the demonstrated victory, later funded Turf fee credits go to the then-entitled Family. Protocol principal stays in controller custody through the takeover; this says nothing about a fixed market value or guaranteed future income.

The film condenses that sequence into physical comedy and a small two-lane overlay. It does not turn the viewer's cargo purchase into an automatic onchain transaction.

## Scope of “full release”

Use the implemented designs and documented boundaries to define these scenes. A launch assumption supplies availability, funding and integration; it does not supply arbitrary new mechanics. In particular, there is still no omniscient rat detector, automatic crafting-to-OMR faucet, universal NFT export, invented tokenomics, or guaranteed earnings. RWA activation and broader profession systems remain outside these three masters because the inspected material does not establish the specific player outcome to dramatize.
