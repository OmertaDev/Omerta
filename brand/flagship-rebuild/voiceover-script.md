# OMERTÀ: Trust Is the Game

Rebuilt narrative, full public-launch premise. 793 words.

## 0–15s · An empty seat

Nico has put up the stake. One seat is empty. Fill it with the wrong person, and everyone could go to jail. This is OMERTÀ, a multiplayer mafia strategy RPG in your browser.

## 15–52s · Your first decision

Nico starts with a name and a little cash. He takes a small job. Nerve goes down. Cash and respect go up. Now what? Another job could build his bankroll. Training spends energy to improve his stats. Banking protects money once it arrives, but the trip leaves it exposed. Three choices, different risks. Nico has to choose what matters next. That is how a place in this city begins: one decision, then its consequence.

## 52–90s · Become useful

As he progresses, six Paths open different ways forward. The Gun brings street combat. The Ledger, fronts and trade. The Kitchen, better cooking. The Wheel, faster convoys. The Shadow, quicker searches. The Ring, stronger duels and bouts. Every advantage costs something. Nico chooses the Wheel: faster convoys, slower cooking. Mara chooses the Gun. Their specialties are different; their preparation still matters. Nico starts thinking beyond his next payout. What can he do that makes someone else want him around?

## 90–125s · A route worth taking

He buys a sack of coffee. There is the price, the debit, and the cargo in his trunk. Getting a bargain is only half the decision. Goods need moving. Cars need repairs. Boats open sea runs with risks of their own. A business has upkeep; a Kitchen batch takes time. Races, boxing, the Den and the Speakeasy give people other reasons to return. Loans, auctions and fixers create other opportunities. The city rewards paying attention.

## 125–178s · Who gets the other seat?

Nico forms a Crew, a small circle for mutual aid and chat. Then comes the Payroll Office job. This heist needs a wheelman and muscle. Nico takes the wheelman role and puts up the stake. The second seat is empty. Mara joins. Both names are there. Preparation improves their odds. It cannot tell Nico what somebody else will choose. He commits. The law was waiting. Somebody talked. Both players go to jail. The result names no informant. Nico can read exactly what happened. He still does not know whom to believe.

## 178–212s · What survives a loss?

Jail ends. The question remains. Would you invite the same person again? Bigger risks can cost more: a character can die. Your account continues, but an heir keeps only a limited legacy. Seasons have their own rules. Eligible gear and vehicles can leave the game, but extracted items stop working inside it. Combat cannot take unrelated assets from your external wallet. What do you keep in play? What are you prepared to lose?

## 212–252s · What do you know?

Elsewhere in the city, discoveries can be kept private or shared with permission. Two investigators may need evidence they found independently. Copying the same clue is not another witness. Objects tell stories too: salvage becomes material, and an eligible recipe makes an item with a traceable history. Beyond Nico's small Crew, Families pursue larger ambitions: treasury, territory, rivalries and wars. Shared operations need contributions and approval. Convoys can be ambushed. Commission seats give votes on specific city rules. Events and the Director's authored situations change the opportunities around you.

## 252–280s · Who is across the street?

Not every rival is at a keyboard. A player can operate an agent. Here it reads its available actions and takes a street job. Nerve falls; cash and respect change. The next decision starts from that new state. Permissions and resources still limit what it can do. The city's NPCs are separate. Whether you bring friends or build an agent, the choices still have consequences.

## 280–350s · What does power cost?

Nico needs equipment, access and people. Money helps, but the balances have different jobs. Game cash pays for city life. In-game OMR buys selected gear and services; some privileges require holding or staking it. Most OMR spends recycle it. Wallet OMR is separate, onchain. The funded Window spends OMR for game cash. That conversion goes one way. Game rewards are not automatic wallet payouts.

Trading fees fund developer, community, asset and liquidity budgets. Inventory bonds release existing OMR over time. Liquidity rewards need eligible positions and funded campaigns. Some realized arbitrage profit can support reserves. Families compete for designated trading-fee rights; they do not take the pool's principal.

Real-world assets include Stock Tokens: exposure to assets outside the game. Commission choices, acquisition, custody and eligibility govern the process. Holding OMR does not promise everyone a payout. These systems shape what players can organize. They cannot decide whom Nico should trust.

## 350–390s · The next invitation

The next job comes round. Another stake. Another empty seat. Nico has learned how to earn, how to specialize, and how much another person's decision can cost him. The next invitation is a choice he has to make. You can buy equipment. You cannot buy trust. Choose your path. Find your people. Make your name. OMERTÀ. Who will you bring?
