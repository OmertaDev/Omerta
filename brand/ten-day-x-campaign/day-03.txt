OMERTÀ's street economy starts with a simple premise: every movement of cash should leave a record someone can reconcile.
An append-only ledger gives the city memory. A nightly conservation sweep asks whether the numbers still add up.
The interesting question is not only how much cash you have. It is where it came from, where it went, and who noticed.
A bank deposit has a journey before it clears. That delay gives timing a price.
Withdrawals can be immediate while deposits are in transit. A player who ignores the difference may plan with cash they cannot yet use.
Would you carry cash into a dangerous district or wait for the bank to clear it first?
The safest ledger entry is often the one made before your next risky move.
District quotes move in four-hour blocks. A price can change while you are still choosing a route.
Arbitrage is a problem in geography and fees, not a magic button marked profit.
Compare a quote, price the trip, count the fees, then ask if the spread still exists.
Two players can see the same market and make different choices because they stand in different districts.
A route that looks clever on paper can fail on a clock. The city charges for delay.
Rackets accrue on your clock. Their pressure accrues too.
Owning a corner is an operating problem: defend it, maintain it, and know who sees the money move.
Passive in-game income is still exposed to the city's raids, rivals, and upkeep.
The best racket may be the one you can actually protect after nightfall.
Five upgradeable businesses turn an empire into a series of maintenance decisions.
A business is more than a cash flow. It is a place with costs, attention, and somebody who might want it.
Would you expand one front or strengthen the one already paying the bills?
The Kitchen links inputs, purity, yield, stealth, crews, wages, and raids to one clock.
Product does not move itself. A crew gets paid whether or not your plan worked.
Making more can mean exposing more. The Kitchen rewards a manager who can count risk as well as output.
Stealth and yield pull on different parts of the same operation. Choose a recipe for the night you actually have.
The black market needs another side to every deal: buyers, sellers, reserves, and clocks.
An anti-snipe clock turns the final moments of an auction into a decision, not a reflex test.
A buy order says what you are willing to pay. The city decides whether anyone wants that trade.
What would you list at auction if you knew the wrong bidder could also learn what you own?
Credit in OMERTÀ has a direction. A loan can be collateralized, defaulted, and sold.
The dangerous part of lending is not the signature. It is the next week of the borrower's life.
Collateral changes who carries the risk; it does not make risk disappear.
A default becomes someone else's opportunity, problem, or grudge depending on who owns the paper.
Convoys put bulk freight on a real clock. Guards, tolls, insurance, and ambush risk travel with the cargo.
The cheapest route is not always the route that gets a shipment home.
Would you buy more guards, pay the toll, or accept the risk and keep the margin?
Insurance is a cost paid before the bad night, not a story told after it.
Freight makes a district price actionable only if you can actually move the goods.
The port is another logistics map: boats, routes, contraband, interception, and rendezvous.
Water can look open from shore. A route still has chokepoints and people watching it.
Buying a boat is the start of a plan. Upgrading it tells the city what kind of trips you intend to take.
The most valuable harbor contact may be the one who knows when not to sail.
Estate auctions give property, collections, and player consignments a public stage.
A bid is a declaration of value. Winning may also tell rivals what you wanted badly enough to show up for.
An estate can be a home for wealth and a map of what someone thinks is worth keeping.
Collections are personal until they enter a marketplace. Then everyone can see what the story costs.
The Auction House is a place where prestige meets a counterparty with a different plan.
A Family treasury can make power durable only if its books survive the decisions made in its name.
Street Deeds connect ownership to the city's map. The chain facing rail remains gated; check the current status before assuming a live market.
The Megaproject is a reason to think beyond one player's next job. Big plans need records as much as ambition.
