03 PROJECT SUMMARY
What letscash is
letscash is a memecoin launchpad on Uniswap v4. A single transaction deploys an immutable, ownerless token,
creates its pool, and wires in a custom hook that takes a trading fee with a decaying launch premium. The
creator's share of that fee stream is routed to a reward contract chosen at launch: buyback-and-burn of the
token itself, or the platform's own buyback burner.

M-01 MEDIUM FIXED
Exact-output swaps are charged on a smaller fee base than
exact-input, bypassing the launch-window anti-snipe premium
CashCatHook  charges fees in two places. _beforeSwap  handles swaps where ETH is the specified currency (exact-
input buys, exact-output sells) and it takes specifiedAmount × rate  from the gross ETH. _afterSwap  handles the
other side (exact-output buys, exact-input sells) - it takes ethMoved × rate  from the net ETH the pool actually settled.
For the same trade result, the fee bases differ. An exact-input buy pays fee = gross × r , where gross  includes the
fee itself. An exact-output buy pays fee = net × r , where net  is just the pool settlement. The exact-output fee is
always smaller by a factor of (1 - r) .
At baseFeeRate = 10,000  �1%�, the difference is negligible ��0.01%�. At launchFeeRate = 900,000  �90%�, exact-
output buys pay 81% less in fees than exact-input for the same tokens received. This directly undermines the anti-snipe
premium and a sniper routing through exact-output during the launch window captures the launch discount the high fee
was designed to prevent.
The hook is immutable. Lost revenue from launch windows is permanent and uncollectable.
IMPACT
Snipers can bypass the launch-window anti-snipe premium by routing exact-output buys. The effective fee at 90% launch
rate drops from r / (1 - r) = 900%  of the net trade to just r = 90%  - a 10× reduction. Every token launched with a
high decay fee is vulnerable from block one and the hook cannot be patched.
RECOMMENDATION
Charge exact-output (afterSwap) fees on the gross amount rather than the net, matching the effective rate of exact-input
swaps:
�� in _afterSwap, for ETH-unspecified swaps:
uint256 fee = (ethMoved * currentFeeRate(poolId, sender)) / (FEE_DENOMINATOR -
currentFeeRate(poolId, sender));
This makes fee / (ethMoved + fee) = r / FEE_DENOMINATOR , matching the exact-input effective rate.

buyTaxRate() and sellTaxRate() understate the actual fee
during the launch decay window
CashCatToken  stores taxBps  at initialization, derived from baseFeeRate  only - the steady-state fee. The hook
currentFeeRate  actually charges up to launchFeeRate  during the decay window, linearly decaying down to
baseFeeRate  over launchFeeDecay  seconds.
Since launchFeeRate  can be as high as 900,000 pips �90%) and baseFeeRate  can be as low as 10,000 pips �1%�, the
token buyTaxRate()  and sellTaxRate()  views can understate the real fee by up to 90�.
The NatSpec on these functions says "fixed at launch," which is inaccurate when launchFeeDecay �� 0  - the rate
starts high and decays.
IMPACT
Tax scanner terminals and trading bots that read buyTaxRate()  / sellTaxRate()  from the token contract get a stale,
understated fee. Traders relying on these views during the launch window face unexpectedly large slippage. The
_tokenConfig  helper in CashCatFactory  also truncates pips to bps via integer division (baseFeeRate / 100 ),
losing up to 0.99 bps on top.

L-02 LOW FIXED
CashCatSelfBurner.register does not assert key.toId() ==
poolId
CashCatSelfBurner.register  stores _keys[poolId] = key  without verifying that the supplied PoolKey  actually
hashes to the supplied poolId . If they don't match, burn  claims fees from one pool (via hook.claim(poolId) ) and
swaps on a completely different pool (the one defined by key ). This cross-pool confusion is silent and nothing reverts.
The current factory always passes a matching pair because it derives poolId = key.toId()  before calling register .
But the factory is a UUPS proxy and a future upgrade that constructs the arguments differently would silently activate this
path.
The hook register  and the distributor register  don't have this issue because they don't store a separate PoolKey .
IMPACT
If triggered by a future factory upgrade, fees from pool A would fund buy-and-burns on pool B token. Pool A token never
gets burned; pool B token gets burned with someone else fees. The mismatch is permanent since register  is one-shot
(AlreadyRegistered ).
RECOMMENDATION
Add the assertion in register :
function register(PoolId poolId, PoolKey calldata key) external {

Three details determine how much this is worth to an attacker, and each cuts against the size cap being treated as the
mitigation.
The trigger is the attacker. Both entry points are permissionless and pay BOUNTY_BPS , one percent of the chunk. The
attacker does not need to observe a pending transaction or win an ordering race, because they submit the buy
themselves, in one transaction, between their own two trades. They are also paid for doing it.
The per-block gate does not bind what one event extracts. The two lanes hold independent gates, so two chunks are
available per block rather than one, and forcing accrual between calls guarantees each permitted buy is a full cap-sized
chunk, which is the most profitable size to trade against.

IMPACT
Protocol revenue is converted at a price an unprivileged caller chooses, and the difference is theirs. The loss lands on
whichever lane was spent, so either fewer tokens are burned than the burn budget paid for, or the letscash treasury
receives less than it was owed.
What an absolute cap cannot do is bound this, because extraction scales with the buy's size relative to the depth it trades
against, not with its size in ether. Modelling a 0.495 ETH chunk, the cap of 0.5 ETH less the bounty reservation, against a
full-range CASHCAT pool at the 0.3 percent tier with the front-run size swept to its optimum:
pool depth      extracted per buy      share of the buy
5 ETH              0.379 ETH                   76%
25 ETH              0.221 ETH                   45%
50 ETH              0.124 ETH                   25%
100 ETH              0.031 ETH                  6.2%
250 ETH                about 0               about 0
Extraction stays above five percent of every buy until the pool holds roughly 106 ETH at that tier, or roughly 31 ETH at the
one percent tier. Below those depths this is not a rounding loss, it is most of the buy.

contract contains nothing that would notice if the answer changed. Liquidity providers withdrawing is ordinary behaviour

protocol.

second convention.
