[Feynman: Crew.CREW_FIRST_CHARACTER_LOCKS]
Existing-member Crew mutations first hold the Crew record, then the caller's current
character and persistent account, then recheck the account's membership under its row lock.
References: src/crew.js:CREW_FIRST_CHARACTER_LOCKS; src/game.js:withCharacter;
src/server.js registrations of Crew.leaveCrew, kickMember, acceptRequest, setRecruiting.

[Feynman: Crew.kickMember]
The Crew leader can remove another account's membership without locking that account's
character. Reading and locking only the caller character therefore cannot preserve
the caller's Crew disclosure authority against a simultaneous kick.
References: src/crew.js:kickMember; leaveCrew; acceptRequest; acceptInvite.

[Feynman: family removeMember]
Family membership belongs to a character. The caller's character/account locks precede
the Family record and target membership. A boss can remove another character's membership
without locking that target character. Death calls the same removal helper.
References: src/social/gangs.js:removeMember, kickMember, leaveGang, promoteMember;
src/social/estate.js:runEstate; src/social/shared.js:canCommand.
Family promotion updates membership role directly, so a membership SHARE lock also protects
future role-dependent readers against promotion/demotion within their transaction.

[Socratic: knowledge context — why no organization record lock?]
Acquiring a Crew record after runtime.actor would reverse the existing Crew-before-character
order. Knowledge only requires the caller's membership record to preserve read permission;
it does not execute organization actions, change capacity, or require leadership authority.
Crew and Family membership tables have no organization foreign keys that silently add
an organization row lock (schema.sql:crew_members, gang_members).

Agreed Phase 01 lock order:
1. Original current character, then accounts, via src/coordination/runtime.js:actor.
2. Caller crew_members row FOR SHARE, then caller gang_members row FOR SHARE.
3. Target coordination instance where applicable.
4. Relevant claim rows FOR UPDATE in deterministic ID order, then their ACL/grant state.
5. Append claim/link/archive/event/command rows without acquiring any organization,
   foreign character, or foreign account locks afterward.
The knowledge prelock entry is a checked no-op. The context does not lock crews/gangs.
Catalog entries use separate checked transactions to avoid holding different graph claim
batches in a conflicting order against globally ID-sorted board/archive/rebuild readers.
One gate action retains its initial complete bounded candidate set and may add only its
own newly inserted claim during the post-action projection. Rebuild locks the union of
owned claims and archived foreign references before repairing FK-backed projections.
Named-recipient selection reads the recipient identity without locking that other actor.
Sharing freezes the server-resolved recipient principal; current reader membership
determines whether that grant conveys access later.

[Inversion: membership authority]
Attack 1: hold a member's old board, let the leader kick the member, then execute its action.
  Required result: fresh membership revalidation removes the foreign evidence and gate.
Attack 2: start disclosure, remove membership while the read is constructing its projection.
  Required result: the membership removal waits for the held membership row lock, or the
  reader waits for removal and then omits the claim. No snapshot-only durable authorization.
Attack 3: let a Family member die and gain an heir, then read or act using old Family access.
  Required result: the account's own historical claims remain inspectable; old character
  Family grants and old run advancement do not transfer to the heir.

[Feynman: read transaction and revocation]
withPhase2Read uses REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY on real PostgreSQL. It cannot take SHARE locks,
and repeated queries can retain the snapshot that preceded a completed revocation.
Knowledge-sensitive projections use a short checked transaction without domain writes,
acquire membership and exclusive claim locks, and re-read grants after acquiring the claim lock.
Revoke takes the same claim lock; share/revoke update independent ACL revision state.
References: src/content/phase2-transactions.js:withPhase2Read, withPhase2Transaction.

Disclosure linearization boundary:
A checked projection whose authority locks precede a revocation/removal may finish while
that mutation waits. A projection obtaining those locks after the mutation commits must
deny the removed access. Already delivered information cannot be recalled. Responses use
no-store; cached board actions and persisted command receipts grant no future authority.
Receipts omit foreign evidence payloads and link/archive endpoint IDs. An owned-claim
share/revoke receipt can return only that caller's own safe claim.

[CROSS-DOMAIN-DEP: membership writers]
All authority-changing member removal paths must actually delete/update the membership
row read by knowledge. Existing Crew/family kick/leave/death paths were traced above.
[CROSS-DOMAIN-DEP: transaction ownership]
Runtime and knowledge share the same checked client and transaction. No nested transaction
or second connection may be acquired while the authority locks are held.
[CROSS-DOMAIN-DEP: transport receipts]
HTTP idempotency and domain receipts must bind the same account, operation and exact body.
Neither a replayed board nor a pre-reserved HTTP key can authorize a new transition.
