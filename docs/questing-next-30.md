# Thirty further questing improvements

These are proposals for the next iteration, separate from the implemented forty-feature release.

Recommended first: mood-based discovery, the rehearsal room, chain of custody, playable redaction, and a knowledge-based time loop.

1. **Mood-based quest finder.** Filter cases by comedy, mystery, tension, available time, and reading load.
2. **Focused reading mode.** Keep the current conversation visible while hiding unrelated controls and preserving scroll position.
3. **Inline character reminders.** Tap a name anywhere in quest dialogue for a spoiler-safe reminder of your shared history.
4. **Performed letters and conversations.** Offer optional voice performances with transcripts, speed controls, and equivalent access to every clue.
5. **Puzzle-aware localization.** Verify that translated clues preserve the reasoning rather than accidentally depending on English wording.
6. **Voluntary challenge rules.** Choose constraints such as no hints or protecting every witness, with recognition separate from economic rewards.
7. **Selectable reasoning depth.** Offer introductory and advanced editions that add deductions rather than errands.
8. **A rehearsal room.** Practice each interaction type in a fictional exercise before encountering it inside a consequential case.
9. **An alternate-branch archive.** Replay different approaches without rewards while preserving the original canonical decisions.
10. **Writer commentary.** Unlock optional explanations of motives, planted clues, and deliberate ambiguities after completion.
11. **Evidence chain of custody.** Trace every handler and alteration to establish why a particular document can be trusted.
12. **Playable redaction.** Protect identities by removing sensitive details while keeping enough evidence to support the finding.
13. **Controlled experiments.** Test inks, paper, chemicals, or mechanisms in a small simulated lab to distinguish competing hypotheses.
14. **Rumor genealogy.** Trace successive retellings to find where a reliable observation became an accusation.
15. **Musical codes.** Decode melodies or rhythmic signals through equivalent audio, notation, and text controls.
16. **A silent investigation.** Tell a short mystery through gestures, objects, and illustrations, with descriptive accessibility alternatives.
17. **NPC initiative.** Let supporting characters pursue signaled plans as investigation steps advance, independent of real-time schedules.
18. **A knowledge-based time loop.** Replay one fictional evening with retained discoveries, enabling increasingly informed interventions.
19. **A promise ledger.** Track voluntary commitments, choose when to fulfill them, and explicitly renegotiate obligations.
20. **Adversarial case review.** Have an examiner challenge a completed argument with alternative explanations the player must address or concede.
21. **Provisional case closure.** Allow an honest insufficient-evidence finding and define which later discoveries can reopen the case.
22. **One-room bottle episodes.** Publish compact stories with a few strong characters and concentrated discovery in a single location.
23. **A mentor observation seat.** Let an invited expert suggest questions while the learner retains every submission and decision.
24. **Evidence-linked discussions.** Attach shared questions and replies directly to evidence, keeping collaboration arguments easy to follow.
25. **Solvability explanations for authors.** Show conflicting valid solutions or contradictory clues when a workshop mystery cannot be published.
26. **Workshop co-authorship.** Credit collaborators with separate permissions to edit, review, and publish a mystery.
27. **Versioned workshop editions.** Publish corrections while retaining prior editions, their ratings, and version-specific player progress.
28. **Structured workshop reviews.** Rate clarity, fairness, and enjoyment separately so difficulty is not mistaken for poor explanation.
29. **Pinpointed confusion reports.** Let players identify the exact clue or transition that lost them after completing a case.
30. **An investigator curriculum.** Recommend optional cases that teach unfamiliar reasoning techniques based on demonstrated experience.
