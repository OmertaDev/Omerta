Method: dual-agent (A: `/root/design_review` · B: `/root/evidence_audit`)

## Anti-pattern verdict

OMERTÀ does not look like a generic AI-generated SaaS page. The wet-street hero, condensed wordmark, in-character copy, server-state details, and extensive custom imagery create a real point of view. The hero is the strongest visual moment on the site.

It does carry visible generative/template fingerprints: six identical feature cards; repeated stat-card bands; generic noir cues; Arena and Play's tiny tracked kickers plus centered intros; Codex gradient text, repeated numbered section markers, side-stripe callouts, and procedural grain; and a tendency to put every system in a bordered card.

The deterministic scan returned 10 warnings: four `side-tab`, three `em-dash-overuse`, one `bounce-easing`, one `broken-image`, and one `dark-glow`. It missed the Codex gradient masthead, repeated numbered markers, 2px side stripe, procedural `feTurbulence` grain, and Arena's hero-metric grid. Context lowers the severity of several hits: the bounce belongs to a result stamp, the broken image is a hidden tour placeholder populated by JavaScript, colored glows communicate state in a noir game, and em dashes are partly the brand voice. Side stripes and repeated scaffolding are real design-system issues.

No visual overlay is available. Browser mutation was read-only, so the required injection preflight failed; no live server was started and no `[Human]` overlay was presented. CLI findings, live DOM measurements, source inspection, console checks, and eight screenshots were used instead.

## Overall impression

OMERTÀ has a far better product underneath than the public funnel communicates. The interface repeatedly proves that the team understands onboarding, state recovery, economic risk, and return loops. The problem is sequencing: the visitor enters a convincing city, then gets a feature grid, a giant scenic pause, and a token-policy essay; the player then reaches a console whose depth is mitigated by progressive disclosure but still mapped across several concurrent navigation systems.

The largest opportunity is to make the city feel like a place while making each screen behave like a tool. Preserve cinema for arrival, danger, victory, and district identity. Make ordinary banking, upkeep, markets, crews, inventory, and timers calmer, more semantic, and faster.

## What is working

### A real identity worth preserving

The hero composition, self-hosted display face, serif narrative voice, amber/red/steel semantic palette, and concise in-world copy are memorable. “No wallet, no email — one tap,” “the city remembers everything,” “The Line’s Dead,” and “The Heir Rises” are excellent.

### Thoughtful onboarding and resilience

New players see six screens instead of the full city. The app provides an eight-step skippable tour, a single next-action coach, first-visit system intros, remembered drawers and tabs, `/` quick-jump, live cooldowns, input-safe re-rendering, serialized idempotent actions, clear offline recovery, and reduced-motion/Save-Data gates. These are substantive product strengths.

### Honest help and risk communication

The Codex is searchable and deep-linked. The site distinguishes cash and $OMR, repeatedly discloses dormant extraction, maps server errors to in-world recovery copy, and avoids income promises. Trust content exists; it needs better placement and hierarchy.

### Mobile overflow engineering

All four sampled public surfaces avoided page-level horizontal overflow at 390px despite code, long terminology, and tables. This is a real implementation success.

## Priority issues

### 1. [P1] Entry architecture mixes audiences and hides returning players

**What:** The landing simultaneously recruits first-time humans, returning humans, agent operators, crypto skeptics, and token-aware users. There is no shared global header. “Enter as a Ghost,” “Make Your Bones,” and “Continue with X” overlap but do not plainly explain temporary play versus persistent recovery. OAuth sign-in sits near the end of an approximately 3,974px mobile page.

**Why it matters:** Every audience pays for the others’ content. First-timers do not know whether Ghost is a demo or an account type; returning players must pass a complete pitch; agents discover their route inside the sixth feature card.

**Fix:** Add one public shell across Landing, Arena, Play, and Codex: Play, Codex, Arena, Agents, and a quiet Return to your street action. In the hero, label the primary action plainly—Play instantly—with “temporary guest; claim later” below it. Keep “ghost” as flavor, not the only explanation. Put Sign in / recover beside it. Give agents one dedicated route later instead of three inline links.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape` then `$impeccable clarify`.

### 2. [P1] The console exposes too many concurrent maps of the city

**What:** Shipping source defines 30 screens across eight groups. Desktop uses group and screen rails plus a three-column sheet/task/street layout. Mobile can show bottom navigation, a group rail, a screen rail, sticky vitals, top utilities, and the coach together. Full-mode bottom labels are only 8.5px.

**Why it matters:** Grouping is better than 30 flat tabs, but duplicated navigation keeps recall high. Veterans know what they want yet must remember its taxonomy. Mobile chrome competes with the current decision.

**Fix:** Use one persistent navigation model per breakpoint. Desktop: compact left nav with groups, recent/favorite screens, unread/threat badges, and quick-jump; make Sheet and Street collapsible. Mobile: five stable destinations—Home, Streets, Earn, Family, More—with More opening quick-jump. Remove the duplicate rails. Add an obligation queue for collect, upkeep, cooldown-ready, threat, and contract actions. Let the coach link to the exact control, not only the screen.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill` and `$impeccable layout`.

### 3. [P1] Accessibility primitives are missing from the interactive core

**What:** Custom modals are plain divs without dialog semantics, focus containment/return, or consistent Escape behavior. Tabs lack tab roles and selected relationships. Toasts, busy state, outage countdown, search results, async Arena content, and clipboard feedback have no live regions. Several auth and creation inputs rely on placeholders. The clickable Help span is not a button. Many controls are 38–40px, below a 44px mobile target.

Contrast problems include Arena/Play `--dim` on panel at 4.31:1, Codex `--faint` at 3.19–3.36:1, and small dim/italic copy. Codex has zero semantic landmarks; none of the four sampled pages has a `<main>` element. `translate="no"` blocks browser translation even though only menu chrome is localized.

**Why it matters:** Keyboard, screen-reader, low-vision, translation-dependent, and motor-impaired users cannot dependably perceive state or complete the core flow. These failures are structural, not cosmetic.

**Fix:** Build one dialog primitive using native `<dialog>` or fully managed semantics; add focus trap/return and Escape. Implement tab/listbox relationships, `aria-current`, `aria-pressed`, visible labels, descriptions, inline errors, status/live regions, and semantic page landmarks. Raise standalone targets to 44px. Replace low-contrast text tokens and test keyboard-only, NVDA/VoiceOver, 200% zoom, forced colors, RTL, and reduced motion.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit` and `$impeccable harden`.

### 4. [P1] The landing spends its emotional peak on explanation instead of playable proof

**What:** After the strongest hero, users receive six equally weighted cards, a 750KB narrative-free city image, a 90-second Money Map, and three long centered paragraphs defending the severed economy. There is almost no UI proof: no annotated console, live city event, sample decision, player story, turf map, or visible turn.

**Why it matters:** Strategy players see token architecture before a single job. Crypto-skeptical users interpret the defensive length as risk; crypto-native users see extraction repeatedly described as dormant. The site says the city is alive without showing live play.

**Fix:** Re-sequence: hero → one interactive/sample move → live city proof → three distinct pillars → human and agent routes → concise economy diagram with a link to the full model. Use left-aligned short prose. Keep the scene break only if it carries story or live state. Move the full manifesto to the Codex.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`, `$impeccable layout`, and `$impeccable clarify`.

### 5. [P2] Codex, Arena, and Play behave like separate microsites

**What:** The console uses a self-hosted display face, 7–12px radii, rich art, motion, and one amber. Arena/Play use Georgia/Courier, flat 5–6px cards, template kickers, and no persistent navigation. Codex uses a warmer amber, 2px geometry, gradient logo, numbered eyebrows, and a large sidebar. Each recreates tokens inline. There is no canonical `mood.md`, `voice.md`, or `tokens.md`. A raw source inventory found 1,120 CSS-variable uses but also 94 unique hex literals and 1,438 inline-style occurrences in `public/index.html`; these counts demonstrate migration/design-debt scope, not that every literal is invalid.

On mobile, Codex keeps a 44vh nested contents scroller with 41 compact links above a 48,903px document. Arena calls itself live but fetches once, shows no timestamp, and currently gives six zero-like metrics prime space—including extracted $OMR while extraction is dormant. Play promises “no code, no terminal” but requires Node, developer settings, JSON editing, and restart; it suggests replacing a config without a safe merge/validation step.

**Why it matters:** Trust and learnability reset on every route. The flagship agent proof page looks less alive than the landing card linking to it. The most novice-facing setup flow contains the riskiest manual step.

**Fix:** Extract a shared public shell, identity tokens, focus behavior, buttons, status components, content widths, and motion policy. Let each page keep a job-specific composition. Codex: search-first drawer, task paths, responsive tables, semantic content. Arena: meaningful zero state, current city/opportunity data, latest moves, polling plus freshness. Play: “no coding; one config edit,” OS-specific visual steps, safe merge paths, validation, copy failure, and a verified-success ending.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable document`, `$impeccable extract`, `$impeccable adapt`, and `$impeccable onboard`.

### Design-debt leverage order

1. **High severity × high frequency / medium effort:** shared dialog/tab/status/label/focus primitives; contrast tokens; target sizes.
2. **High severity × high frequency / high effort:** one navigation model per breakpoint; obligation queue; public entry architecture.
3. **Moderate severity × high frequency / medium effort:** shared public shell and token tiers; remove inline one-offs during component extraction.
4. **Moderate severity × medium frequency / medium effort:** task-based Codex, live Arena state, verified agent setup.
5. **Low severity / defer to host redesign:** isolated side stripes, bounce curve, and decorative glow/grain cleanups.

## Surface-by-surface findings

### Landing and authentication

- Preserve the hero; it is the brand anchor.
- The supporting copy and Wire ticker become faint over bright moving imagery.
- The `H1` jumps to six `H3` card titles before any `H2`.
- Six feature cards are too equal; the Agent card is overloaded with a status parenthesis and three links.
- The full-width city image consumes scroll without changing the user's mental model.
- Long centered paragraphs are hard to scan; center alignment should stop after two or three lines.
- Guest and persistent sign-in have nearly equal visual treatments but different consequences.
- Character creation needs visible name constraints, uniqueness/availability feedback, and an explicit optional referral treatment.
- Developer provider controls lack visible labels; one live measurement found the provider submit button without an accessible name.
- Footer disclosures are cautious but far from the first on-chain claim.

### Authenticated console, source-inspected

- Current-task-first mobile ordering and sticky vitals are correct.
- The coach is the most valuable information-architecture mechanism; elevate it into a persistent queue/history.
- Let users collapse Sheet and Street when planning or moving money.
- Recast Last Word from raw response log to a collapsible activity/receipt drawer.
- Static cards should not hover/raise as though clickable.
- Risk/primary button styles are good; standardize consequence summaries and confirmation rules.
- First-visit intros should offer the first action, not only “got it.”
- Replace universal hero bands/cards with system-native artifacts: ledger rows, a case timeline, contract sheet, turf map, race card, wanted dossier.
- Expose more shortcuts, recent screens, favorites, and batch maintenance for veterans.

### Codex

- Desktop search, deep links, active tracking, reading measure, and no-results copy are strong.
- Mobile contents should be a drawer; the current nested scroller consumes nearly half the viewport.
- Search needs result count, snippets/highlighting, a clear action, and live announcement; empty group headings should disappear.
- Newcomers need task routes: First 10 minutes, Make money, Stay alive, Join people, Understand $OMR, Run an agent.
- Remove decorative numbering, gradient masthead, and side-stripe callouts. Use actual OMERTÀ artifacts.
- Add semantic nav/main landmarks and mobile table transforms/overflow affordances.
- Move content from a massive JavaScript array toward semantic HTML/Markdown for indexing and no-JS resilience.

### Arena

- Wealth banding is a smart safety/gameplay decision.
- The current zero state makes the city look abandoned. Hide meaningless metrics and invite a first mover without fabricating activity.
- Replace dormant extraction with active metrics: opportunities open, actions today, city event, widest spread, contracts open.
- Add “updated N seconds ago,” connection state, polling, and latest activity.
- Prepare the seven-column leaderboard for mobile using ranked rows/cards or explicit horizontal scrolling.
- Separate human-readable stories, machine-readable endpoints, and executable setup links.
- Remove indefinite pulse or respect reduced motion.

### Agent setup

- Three numbered steps are clear and legitimately sequential.
- Change the promise to “No coding; one small configuration edit.”
- Split Mac/Windows with screenshots and step-local troubleshooting.
- Never lead with “replace whatever is in the file”; show safe merge paths and backup/validation.
- Copy buttons measured about 55×22px; raise touch size and announce success/failure.
- Add a connection test and celebratory verified-success state.
- Move other-model/background material after success or into audience links.

## Cognitive load

Six of eight checklist items fail end-to-end: single focus, chunking, visual hierarchy, one thing at a time, minimal choices, and working-memory support. Grouping and progressive disclosure pass. Major decision points exceed four options on the landing feature set, full console navigation, mobile navigation stack, Codex contents, Arena metrics/links, and language selection.

The task itself is genuinely deep; that intrinsic load is part of the game. The redesign should remove extraneous load: duplicated navigation, unexplained jargon, universal card styling, separated contextual help, and parallel panels. Preserve germane load: the coach, meaningful groupings, progressive unlocks, consequences, and feedback that help users master the city.

## Persona red flags

### Jordan — first-time human

- Cannot immediately distinguish temporary Ghost play from a persistent account.
- Encounters $OMR, RICO, staking, extraction, agents, and two-economy policy before making one move.
- First likely form error is a name constraint the UI never stated.
- Receives an eight-step tour before the first job.
- Meets several concurrent rails after onboarding.
- The Codex offers dozens of system choices instead of a first-ten-minutes path.

### Alex — returning power player

- A lost local session sends them through the entire landing before OAuth sign-in.
- `/` quick-jump, saved state, timers, and protected forms are excellent accelerators.
- Repeated collection/upkeep/claim actions lack a unified obligation queue.
- The live Street competes with planning; persistent panels are not collapsible.
- Only one visible shortcut exists beyond API automation.
- Arena cannot be left open as a reliable live spectator page.

### Sam — keyboard, screen-reader, or low-vision user

- Help, tabs, modal states, toasts, busy state, selected filters, and async updates are not consistently semantic.
- Placeholder-only fields and missing error associations destabilize forms.
- Several standalone targets are under 44px.
- Small dim text fails or nearly fails contrast on Arena/Play/Codex.
- Mixed English prose plus translated chrome and `translate="no"` block an understandable localization strategy.
- Strong focus-visible styling and keyboard quick-jump are good foundations to preserve.

## Performance and implementation clues

- `public/index.html` is approximately 1.08MB uncompressed and contains public landing plus all authenticated CSS/JavaScript inline. Split and cache the public shell; lazy-load game systems and non-visible art.
- Hero motion is well gated and falls back to a still.
- Shipped media includes a 1.49MB autoplay hero clip, 750KB scene break, and 53.9MB Money Map video. The large video uses `preload="metadata"`, which is correct; disclose duration/data and provide adaptive delivery if played on mobile.
- Add responsive images and lazy-loading. Preserve Save-Data and reduced-motion behavior.
- The global action lock and idempotency key are excellent error prevention. Show loading on the initiating control rather than only a global cursor/busy strip.

## Minor observations

- Arena and Play need branded focus-visible treatment.
- Arena needs a table caption and numeric alignment; sorting should be semantic if added.
- Hide collective wealth when population is zero.
- Play swallows clipboard failures; provide manual recovery.
- Use `overflow-wrap:anywhere` rather than `word-break:break-all` for paths.
- Standardize public link names: The City, Play, Play it yourself, and Human console currently describe the same destination.
- Indicate new-tab behavior in accessible text.
- Make menu-only localization explicit in visible UI, not a title tooltip; test RTL with English prose.
- Apply hover elevation only to interactive cards.
- Replace arbitrary high z-index values with semantic tokens.
- Stop the breathing CTA after its first few seconds; Arena pulse should honor reduced motion.
- The Wire ticker truncates the city's most interesting proof. Prefer a small list or context-aware live item.
- Empty/outage/status components should share the strong “The Line’s Dead” pattern across the product.
- No sampled page produced horizontal page overflow at 390px; preserve this.

## Questions to consider

1. If $OMR vanished from the landing until after a player's first job, would conversion improve without reducing informed consent?
2. Which audience deserves the first viewport: a new human, a returning human, or an agent operator?
3. What one live artifact proves this is a game, not a noir-themed token project?
4. Does a veteran need 30 destinations, or a queue of obligations, threats, opportunities, and recent places?
5. What would Arena celebrate if extraction were not a metric?
6. Which visual artifacts exist only in OMERTÀ, and can they replace generic noir silhouettes, metric cards, and tiny tracked kickers?
7. Can every irreversible action answer, in place: what will I spend, what can I lose, and how do I recover?
