# OMERTÀ max-effort UI/UX critique — 2026-08-24

## Scope and confidence

This assessment combines two isolated reviews, live production inspection of `/`, `/play`, `/wiki`, `/arena`, and the public `/admin` gate at desktop and mobile widths, source inspection of the authenticated console, a local 83-screen mobile harness, page-weight measurement, design-token analysis, content review, and detector evidence. No source or production state was changed.

Evidence is labeled deliberately:

- **Observed live:** directly seen or measured on `https://www.omerta.fun` without creating an account.
- **Observed source/workspace:** verified in the current repository or local browser harness; it may be ahead of or differ from production.
- **Inference:** a design conclusion supported by those observations, but not yet validated by player research or analytics.

The authenticated production console and privileged admin UI were not entered. Recommendations for those surfaces are based on source, local harness results, and the documented design contract.

## Executive verdict

OMERTÀ already has something most game sites never achieve: a recognizable authored world. The warm-paper, black-lacquer, gold, serif/mono, dossier-and-ledger system is specific; the copy sounds like one city rather than a generic dark dashboard; and risky actions communicate consequences unusually well. The site should not be “redesigned” into a cleaner SaaS shell. Its noir-documentary language is the asset.

The next leap is editorial and systemic: make the first experience about identity, conflict, and consequence before token infrastructure; make every apparent live signal truthful; let mature players navigate by intent rather than a 30-screen taxonomy; raise operational readability; and move the interface from a one-megabyte inline implementation to governable components and tokens. On mobile, fix the current-workspace short-viewport fold regression before adding visual polish.

**Overall heuristic score: 30/40.** Good foundations, material prioritization and density problems, no observed P0 blocker.

### AI-slop verdict

**Overall pass, with localized failures.** The product is too coherent and specific to read as a generated template. The clearest template pocket is Arena: six equal KPI cards, especially when every value is zero. Repeated equal-weight cards, tiny uppercase tracked labels, and the bright Money Router infographic also weaken the otherwise authored register. Detector findings were reviewed rather than accepted blindly: one side-stripe card treatment in `fee-flows.html` is a genuine visual cliché; the selected-destination stripe is contextual; aggregate em-dash count is not a route-level defect; and the nitrous glow is meaningful armed-state feedback, not decorative glow.

## What is working and should be protected

1. **The brand has a point of view.** The visual system moves credibly from cinematic public thresholds to documentary operations. The display serif, monospaced data, physical artifacts, red/gold accents, and district language form a memorable world.
2. **The opening promise is clear.** “One city · every move on the books” and the prominent guest CTA communicate persistence and low commitment quickly.
3. **Consequences are designed, not buried.** Costs, permanence, forfeiture, recovery, and reversibility are often colocated with the decision. Danger dialogs use `alertdialog`, focus trapping, Escape, restored focus, and cancellation-first defaults.
4. **The onboarding foundation is excellent.** Character creation is brief, the tour is only two steps, the server coach names one best move, simple mode reduces the early surface, and veterans get quick jump and remembered navigation.
5. **Help is unusually complete.** Codex, glossary, machine endpoints, agent setup, contextual intros, and stable error semantics make both human and agent play legible.
6. **Accessibility has real foundations.** Skip links, tab semantics, keyboard tab movement, live regions, `aria-busy`, reduced-motion rules, forced-color support, and copy-status announcements are present.
7. **Public responsiveness mostly holds.** Live Wiki, Arena, Play, and mobile Home had no page-level horizontal overflow at the measured mobile width. Code samples scroll within their own containers.
8. **Truth around the dormant rail is commendably explicit.** Preserve this. It is a trust advantage in a product with a ledgered economy.

## Ranked findings

### P1. The homepage Wire blurs fiction and authoritative live truth

**Observed live/source:** The rotating “THE WIRE” ticker uses curated client-side lines, while “Wire” is also a real in-game surface. Arena simultaneously showed no agents and zero activity. The product repeatedly promises server authority and receipts.

**Inference:** A visitor can reasonably read those lines as real player events. That weakens the strongest trust claim in the brand.

**Redesign:** Choose one truth mode. Either populate a sanitized real-event feed and mark it `LIVE`, with timestamp and empty state, or rename the fictional device to **Scenes from the city** and style it as editorial atmosphere. Never make fictional flavor look like ledger evidence.

### P1. The current workspace loses selected gameplay content below the fold on short phones

**Observed workspace:** The 83-screen mobile harness found 28 failures at 375×667. After selecting nearly every mature gameplay tab, its first real content began around y=695–701, below the viewport. The same checks passed at 360×780; there was no horizontal-overflow or tap-navigation failure.

**Boundary:** This is verified in the current working tree, not in an authenticated production session. Previous production QA may differ.

**Redesign:** Treat the operation itself as the fold priority. At ≤700px height, collapse or coalesce the title plate, vitals, coach, rail, and group tabs; scroll the selected panel heading into view; keep only one persistent status strip; and make the first actionable control visible without an extra scroll. Add 320×568 and 375×667 to the deployment gate.

### P1. Operational typography violates the product’s own readability contract

**Observed source:** `DESIGN.md` establishes a 14px operational-copy floor. `index.html` contains 26 distinct pixel sizes, 217 occurrences of 11px, many 10–12.5px declarations, and mobile labels as small as 8.5px. `--om-text-faint` is about 4.34:1 on the canonical panel and about 4.14:1 on the raised surface, below 4.5:1 for normal text; it is used by 10px labels.

**Inference:** The densest, most important screens are the most tiring for low-vision, older, interrupted, and mobile players. Tiny uppercase tracking compounds the problem.

**Redesign:** Enforce six semantic sizes rather than 26 ad hoc values. Use 16px public body, 14–16px operational copy, 12px only for nonessential metadata, and at least 12–13px for bottom-navigation labels. Retire faint text for anything actionable or explanatory. Test at 200% zoom, forced colors, and low brightness.

### P1. Mature navigation requires memorizing the implementation taxonomy

**Observed source:** Full mode exposes 30 screens across eight groups. Some groups contain six destinations; Streets can show six travel choices, eight jobs with three approaches, and six permanent career paths. Quick jump, coach, recent state, and simple mode mitigate this, but jump primarily expects exact screen names or IDs.

**Inference:** Experts can become fast, but only after learning where the designer filed each system. Less-fluent players become coach-dependent instead of learning a stable city model.

**Redesign:** Prototype a five-intent shell—**Now, Earn, Fight, People, Assets**—without deleting expert destinations. Keep the 30 routes as searchable deep links. Make `/` synonym-aware (`heal`, `sell car`, `take loan`, `find work`), add Recent and Frequent, and allow pinning two destinations. Preserve one dominant coach recommendation rather than adding more cards.

### P1. The landing payload and entrance choreography tax the highest-leverage moment

**Observed workspace/live:** A cold 375×667 local load transferred about **4.216 MB**, led by a 1.49 MB hero video, 750 KB break image, 645 KB poster, and a 328 KB compressed/1.087 MB decoded HTML document. Entering the game added about **1.725 MB**. Live mobile screenshots showed primary hero content initially opacity-gated by staggered entrance animation; it later appeared and reduced motion disables the animation. Live desktop Home had a measured 5px horizontal overflow caused by full-bleed `100vw` math.

**Inference:** Fast localhost paint does not predict a budget phone on cellular. The interface makes visitors pay media and animation cost before the primary proposition is fully available.

**Redesign:** Render headline and CTA visible in the first frame; animate decoration, not comprehension. Use an AVIF/WebP poster, responsive `srcset`, explicit media dimensions, and load video only after intent or when the network/device budget allows. Replace `100vw` breakout math with container-safe full bleed. Target <1.5 MB for the first public viewport and <250 KB of critical HTML/CSS/JS.

### P1. The design system cannot govern a monolithic, inline implementation

**Observed source:** `index.html` is 1,087,066 bytes and 10,988 lines, with 1,421 inline style attributes, 502 pixel font-size declarations, 42 pixel radii, and 13 distinct z-index values. It contains most gameplay rendering and copy. The shared `--om-*` token layer is thoughtful, but the main console frequently bypasses it through legacy aliases and inline literals.

**Inference:** Visual regressions, cross-surface drift, and inconsistent states will recur because the token system is advisory rather than structural.

**Redesign:** Extract the shell, navigation, artifact card, action row, ledger/table, field, dialog, toast, empty state, and status strip as governed primitives. Replace literal colors/sizes/z-index values with semantic tokens; lint new inline styles and unapproved font sizes; add visual QA fixtures for each state. Do this incrementally by highest-reuse component, not as a risky rewrite.

### P2. The page explains the token economy before proving the game fantasy

**Observed live:** After a strong hero and “first moves” sequence, the largest post-hero artifact is a bright Money Router/Map and economy explainer. It is visually disconnected from the noir surfaces. Identity, rivalry, crew work, and consequence receive less concrete proof.

**Inference:** First-time humans may leave remembering crypto plumbing rather than the fantasy of becoming a feared operator. The economy is credible, but it arrives before emotional investment.

**Redesign:** Reorder the narrative: hero → truthful city pulse → interactive first operation → consequence receipt/rival dossier → human/agent routes → social proof → “how the books work.” Move the complete flow map to Codex or a collapsed auditor’s section. If it stays public, redraw it as an intentional ledger/dossier insert rather than a white fintech infographic.

### P2. Codex is comprehensive but not task-shaped

**Observed live:** Wiki contains about 64,000 characters, 49 headings, and a 36,500px desktop/50,900px mobile document. Desktop has nested page and contents scrolling. On mobile, search is behind Browse Sections. A search for “death” returned 16 sections without match snippets.

**Inference:** Completeness has become navigation work. Players know their question (“How do I heal?”), not necessarily its category or canonical term.

**Redesign:** Keep a canonical source, but publish it as a task-based hub with category and article routes. Put search above the fold on mobile; return highlighted snippets and direct anchors; add “What do you want to do?” intents, related rules, last-updated/state badges, and contextual links from each gameplay operation. Preserve a single-page/downloadable reference for power users and agents.

### P2. Arena turns an exciting promise into six repetitions of zero

**Observed live:** Six equal metric cards—agents, identities, bodies, wealth, extracted OMR, kills—consume much of the mobile fold even when all values are zero. The useful “be the first machine” message appears afterward.

**Inference:** This is the most generic dashboard surface and converts lack of activity into six pieces of negative social proof.

**Redesign:** Until data is meaningful, show one city-ledger empty artifact with the conditions for the first ranked entry, a setup CTA, current system status, and perhaps a chronological activity strip. Reveal comparative metrics only when they tell a story. When populated, lead with named rivals and movement, not totals.

### P2. Affordance and risk rules are strong but not formalized across every verb

**Observed source:** Many destructive decisions use excellent consequence confirmations. Some `.risk` actions—such as “go loud,” “fire hit,” “rob trunk,” “sabotage,” or combat moves—do not visibly use the same confirmation path. Some are intentionally frequent actions with terms shown inline, so absence of a dialog is not automatically a defect.

**Inference:** The system needs a declared risk taxonomy; otherwise visual red can mean danger, commitment, or simply genre emphasis, and confirmation coverage will drift.

**Redesign:** Define four levels: reversible/no-cost actions execute immediately; low-cost actions show inline terms and undo where possible; high-cost/rare actions require a review step; destructive/permanent actions use `alertdialog`, exact loss, reversibility, and cancel-first focus. For high-frequency combat, prefer an explicit armed mode or two-step commit over repeated modal friction.

### P2. Form and recovery quality is uneven at the first identity moment

**Observed source:** Character creation has explicit constraints but is not a native form and reports failures through a global toast rather than field-local messaging. The city-chat input has placeholder text but no reliable programmatic label. Numeric flows often rely on server rejection after permissive parsing.

**Redesign:** Use real forms; connect hint/error text with `aria-describedby`; focus the first invalid field; preserve entered values; state valid ranges before submission; add an error summary only for multi-field flows. Give city chat a persistent label or `aria-label`. Keep server error codes, but map them to the field/action that caused them.

### P2. Public accessibility misses are small but systemic

**Observed live/source:** Several inline API/footer links on Arena and Play have visual boxes below 44px; bottom-nav labels are too small; faint metadata can fail contrast. The homepage ticker continues changing in JavaScript even when CSS motion is reduced and has no pause control. Strong focus, skip links, semantics, dialogs, and live regions already exist.

**Redesign:** Expand standalone mobile hit areas to 44×44 without making prose links awkward; raise label sizes; fix faint contrast; stop auto-rotation for `prefers-reduced-motion` or provide pause/previous controls; test keyboard, 200% zoom, VoiceOver/TalkBack, forced colors, and `prefers-reduced-data`.

### P3. Smaller, high-value refinements

- Rename the guest CTA from “Play now—no account” to the more exact **Start a guest street—no signup**, then state immediately what is browser-bound and how to preserve it.
- Move optional referral attribution until after the first successful operation; identity creation should feel like a ritual, not acquisition plumbing.
- Pair world terms with plain descriptors on first use: **The Wire — activity**, **OMR — ledger balance**, **Made — inducted status**, **The Window — one-way redemption**.
- Keep Claude as the fastest `/play` path, but give “any MCP client” and “raw JSON/OpenAPI” equal credibility in the page hierarchy.
- Add a route back to the public site from the admin gate while preserving its deliberately restricted presentation.
- Review `fee-flows.html`: its destination-total side stripe is a confirmed cliché and the page appears visually/orchestrationally detached from the current public navigation.
- Reduce repeated card grids. Use ledgers, timelines, dossiers, maps, receipts, and split panes when those structures better match the information.
- Treat the same rainy teal/amber hero scene as a limited threshold motif. Build district identity through lighting, paper stock, stamps, maps, and artifacts so the world does not become one repeated AI-noir image.

## Redesign direction by surface

### Desktop public site

Keep the hero, but make the first 30 seconds a playable narrative. The ideal sequence is: identity promise, truthful live city pulse, one operation with three approaches, a visible consequence receipt, a rival/crew hook, then routes for human and agent players. Move deep economy architecture later. Vary composition—full-bleed scene, ledger excerpt, split dossier, timeline—instead of repeating same-weight cards.

### Mobile public site

Show headline and CTA immediately with no opacity gate. Use a lightweight responsive poster; defer video. Keep a single 44px primary action and compact trust proof. Make Explore disclosure and inline API/footer targets comfortably tappable. Ensure every public route works at 320px width and with 200% zoom.

### Onboarding

Use a native character form with inline validation. Explain guest persistence before commitment, then defer the referrer field. Give the player one first operation, one exact consequence, and one next choice. Unlock new destinations through a short “street opens up” sequence rather than revealing six equal tabs at once. Measure time to first successful crime, abandonment by step, and error recovery.

### Gameplay operations

Keep costs, odds, timers, and recovery next to the action. On desktop, use a stable three-zone frame: current objective, operation workspace, compact state/ledger. On mobile, the operation must begin above the fold after tab selection. Add intent-aware command search, Recents, Frequent, and two pins. Favor ledgers for comparisons, timelines for clocks, and dossiers for targets; reserve cards for genuinely discrete objects.

### Risky and destructive actions

Publish and implement the four-level risk matrix. Make high-consequence review screens name what will happen, what is lost, when it happens, and whether it can be undone. Keep cancel visually safe and focused by default. For repeat combat, arm the action explicitly and display the current target/cost in a persistent commit bar. Log the final result as a receipt.

### Arena

Design this as the city’s public ledger, not an analytics dashboard. Empty: one artifact, system state, qualifying rules, setup path. Populated: lead with rank movement, named rivalries, recent feats, and provenance; place aggregate metrics below. Keep extraction status visibly dormant until it changes.

### Codex

Create a task-based hub, category pages, and direct article anchors from gameplay. Keep a generated canonical dataset so human pages, machine rules, and error copy cannot drift. Search should accept player language, show snippets, and surface current availability/status. Retain the long-form reference as an expert view.

### Admin

The public key gate is focused and appropriately austere. Add a visible return path, persistent field label, precise authentication error, rate-limit/retry state, and session-lock status. Inside, separate read-only monitoring from mutating controls, require contextual confirmation for production changes, and always show target environment and audit receipt.

### Accessibility

Fix typography and contrast first; they affect every screen. Then address city-chat labeling, field-local errors, ticker pause/reduced-motion behavior, mobile hit areas, zoom reflow, and low-data media behavior. Maintain the strong dialog, focus, live-region, forced-color, and tab foundations. Add automated axe/contrast checks plus manual keyboard, VoiceOver/TalkBack, 200%/400% zoom, reduced motion, reduced data, and forced-colors scenarios.

## Recommended design-system and QA program

1. Define semantic component tokens for surface, type, action priority, risk, status, spacing, radius, motion, and z-order; keep legacy aliases only during migration.
2. Build a small pattern library with every primitive in default, hover, focus, pressed, busy, success, empty, error, disabled, and forced-color states.
3. Extract shared primitives from the monolith one at a time, starting with action rows, cards/ledgers, fields, and status strips.
4. Add automated rules: no new inline `style`, no unapproved font size, no low-contrast semantic pair, one primary action per operation, and dialog requirements for destructive risk.
5. Gate releases at 320×568, 360×780, 375×667, 390×844, 768×1024, 1280×720, and 1440×900; include keyboard and 200% zoom.
6. Set budgets: first public viewport <1.5 MB, critical HTML/CSS/JS <250 KB, no opacity-gated CTA, no page-level horizontal overflow, and no selected operation beginning below the short-phone fold.
